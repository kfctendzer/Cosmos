01Seeing the fourth dimension

We can't picture higher dimensions directly, so we project them — the way a 3D cube casts a 2D shadow. Pick a dimension from 2 to 6, drag the sliders to spin in planes no 3D object can access, or click the shape itself to pause.

The math projection

rotate in XW: x′ = x·cosθ − w·sinθ, w′ = x·sinθ + w·cosθ
project: f = d / (d − w), then (x·f, y·f, z·f)

A 4D rotation picks two axes at a time — there are 6 such planes (XY, XZ, XW, YZ, YW, ZW). The sliders above rotate in the three planes that involve W, the axis you can't point to.

16 vertices · 32 edges · 24 faces · 8 cubes

Worked example counts

Vertices: each of 4 coordinates is ±1 → 2⁴ = 16.
Edges: from each vertex, 4 directions to flip one sign → 16·4/2 = 32.
Go higher: a 5-cube has 2⁵ = 32 vertices and 5·32/2 = 80 edges; a 6-cube has 64 vertices, 192 edges. Rule: N = 2ⁿ vertices, E = n·2ⁿ⁻¹ edges.
Hypersphere: x² + y² + z² + w² = r². Slice at fixed w and you see an ordinary 3D sphere grow then shrink — a 4D ball passing through our space.

Topology asks what survives stretching and bending: holes, twists, one-sidedness. Spin the surfaces and compare their invariants.

An ant walking a Möbius strip returns mirrored — one side only. A coffee cup and a donut are the same topologically (one hole, χ = 0).

Worked example

χ of a torus: wrap a 4×4 grid into a tube, then join the ends: V=16, E=32, F=16 → χ = 16−32+16 = 0.
Cut test: cut a torus loop → cylinder. Cut a Möbius loop down the middle → one longer loop, still joined.

03Trigonometry on the unit circle

Sine and cosine are just the coordinates of a point riding the unit circle. Drag the dot — the graphs, identities, and triangle ratios all fall out.

sin 0.64cos 0.76tan 0.84

The math

point: (cos θ, sin θ) · sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
law of sines: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R
law of cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos C

Tangent is the slope of the radius. When the radius points straight up, cosine hits zero and tangent is undefined — the vertical asymptote you see in y = tan x.

Worked example

Height of a tree: stand 20 m back, sight the top at 35°. Height = 20·tan 35° ≈ 20 × 0.700 = 14.0 m.
Check the identity: θ = 40° → sin² + cos² ≈ 0.413 + 0.587 = 1.000 — it always closes because x² + y² = 1.

05Determinism that still surprises

The Lorenz system is fully deterministic — no randomness anywhere — yet nearby starts diverge exponentially. Tune ρ across the boundary from order into chaos.

The equations

dx/dt = σ(y−x) · dy/dt = x(ρ−z)−y · dz/dt = xy−βz
classic: σ=10, β=8/3, ρ=28 → strange attractor

At ρ < 24.7 trajectories spiral into fixed points. Above it, the fixed points go unstable and the butterfly emerges. Laplace's demon — perfect prediction from perfect data — dies on the second decimal place.

Logistic map: chaos in one line

Try it: r = 3.2 → 2-cycle. r = 3.5 → 4-cycle. r = 3.9 → chaos. Rule: xₙ₊₁ = r·xₙ(1−xₙ).

Worked example: teaspoon mass

ρ ≈ M / (4/3 πR³) ≈ 2.8e30 kg / 7e12 m³ ≈ 4 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³

Why it spins so fast: angular momentum L = Iω is conserved while R shrinks 10⁵× — like an ice skater pulling in, squared. A lazy 25-day solar spin becomes milliseconds.
Black hole vs neutron star: both start as collapsing cores. The dividing line (Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff limit) sits near 2.2 M☉ — below it neutrons hold, above it nothing does.

11The particle zoo

Everything is built from 6 quarks, 6 leptons, and the bosons that boss them around. Filter by family, then tap any card for its story.

Worked example: build a proton

u (+2/3) + u (+2/3) + d (−1/3) = +1. Two ups and a down, glued by gluons — that charge arithmetic is why atoms exist.
Beta decay: d → u + W⁻ turns a neutron into a proton — the weak force transmuting elements inside stars.

Reading the table

Top border color = family: blue quarks, purple leptons, gold bosons. Spin-½ particles are matter; integer-spin particles are force carriers — except the Higgs (spin 0), which is a field excitation giving others mass.
